What happens on the server when 30 people use the Rates page

An explainer for the Rates page priced by alpha (resident worker, decided 2026-11-17) with 30 people live at once: how the client, the server and the analytics work together, where waits can arise, and how to avoid them. 2026-11-17. 日本語

In short

Corrected premise (Akira, 2026-11-17 16:40): what production delivers every second is not quotes but finished curves together with the par-delta Jacobians. The worker only reads them and builds neither. This explainer follows that premise.

  • Thirty people's calculations are little CPU. Per second, reading curves and Jacobians (shared by everyone) takes a few ms, and valuation for everyone tens of ms. One resident worker should be enough.
  • Delays come less from calculation than from four places: everyone waiting for curves to be read, sending every cashflow table to the worker on every tick, the server's calculation slots (4 today), and delivery to and drawing in the browser.
  • All four are avoided by design: read curves once when the market arrives; send trades only when their revision changes; on a tick send only "which snapshot, which revision".
  • If the worker stops, nothing switches to a fake calculation: lines show as not priced and the last values keep their time (CLAUDE.md).

2. One tick

  1. The market source makes snapshot n (every second).
  2. The server first hands the market to the worker (market). The worker reads the delivered curves and Jacobians once, however many people there are.
  3. RatesLiveService starts "price on n" for each running subscription. One still busy only keeps n waiting (and drops it if n+1 comes).
  4. When a slot is free, AlphaRatesPricing asks the worker for "snapshot n, this workspace's lines at revision r".
  5. The worker answers per line with PV, par, zero delta and par delta, or a refusal with its reason (a missing fixing, for example).
  6. The server makes a frame and pushes it over SSE to the pages watching that workspace. A page that falls behind gets only the newest frame.
  7. The browser updates the grid. The zero/par switch only picks which of the two delivered sets to show.

Assumptions (estimates): 30 people, 45 live workspaces in all, 20 lines each, a market a second.

5. Price and Capture

  • Price (manual) values once on the snapshot the user saw and saves a Run. If the worker no longer holds that snapshot (it keeps the last 8), the server sends the same snapshot again first; it never values on a newer market instead.
  • Capture saves a frame already shown (the last 32) and does not call the worker.
  • An edited line is valued at its new revision from the next calculation: at worst one in flight plus one more.

6. What in today's design is weak, and what to do

Verdict: the per-subscription rules (one in flight, newest waits), no Run per tick, and SSE delivery fit 30 people and need no change. The weak points are three made on the assumption that calculation runs in the same process, and each can be fixed inside the Rates module without touching the kernel.

So what 30 people need is three changes inside the Rates module (send less, prepare the market first, make the slots a setting), included in the ticket that builds AlphaRatesPricing.

7. What to build in

  1. When the market arrives, have the worker read curves and Jacobians once (shared by all).
  2. Send lines only on a new revision; ticks send ids only.
  3. The worker takes requests by id and serves them in parallel.
  4. The number of slots is a setting, matched to the worker's parallelism.
  5. The market feed has the worker prepare the market before fanning out.
  6. Deliver over HTTP/2 with compression; draw only changed cells.
  7. Record timings: per tick, curve read time, wait, calculation and delivery (qube already has OpenTelemetry).

Figures are estimates except the 12 µs for a 10y swap (measured with alpha's pieces, Xeon 2.1 GHz). Measurements: alpha-for-rates.en.html.
